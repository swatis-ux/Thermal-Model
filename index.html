Why they differ

Air temperature is one input among several.

A weather app measures the air. Your body is exposed to more than air — it exchanges heat with everything around it.

01 — Air temperature

How warm the air is

The number on the app. It describes the surrounding air only, measured in shade, away from direct radiant surfaces.

02 — Humidity

How easily sweat evaporates

Water vapour in the air changes how effectively your body can cool itself through evaporation.

03 — Wind

How heat moves off the body

Moving air strips the thin warm boundary layer around skin, changing the rate of heat transfer.

04 — Mean Radiant Temperature

What's radiating at you

The combined radiant effect of the sun, sky, and every hot or cool surface facing your body.

What a surface actually does

Not "asphalt = +8°C." A chain of physical properties.

No single number describes what a material contributes. What matters is a chain of effects, and the chain runs through several stages before it reaches you.

Albedo
Fraction of solar radiation a surface reflects rather than absorbs.
Emissivity
How efficiently a surface re-radiates absorbed heat as long-wave radiation.
Thermal mass
How much heat a material stores, and how slowly it releases it after sunset.
Conductivity
How quickly heat moves through the material itself.
Surface temperature
The actual measured temperature of the material's surface, not the air above it.
Moisture
Wet or vegetated surfaces lose energy to evaporation instead of heating up.
Shading
Whatever blocks direct solar radiation from reaching a surface or a person.
Geometry
The shape and orientation of surrounding surfaces — a narrow street traps radiation differently than an open field.

Trees aren't simply "cool materials." Their effect is a bundle: shade, evapotranspiration, lower surface temperature, and sometimes reduced airflow — which can work against the cooling in poorly ventilated pockets.

Putting it together

UTCI: an equivalent-temperature index, not a body temperature.

The Universal Thermal Climate Index combines air temperature, humidity, wind and Mean Radiant Temperature into one number, expressed as an equivalent temperature. It describes thermal stress on a standardised body — not your skin temperature, and not a promise of how comfortable any individual will feel.

Scientific honesty

What kind of number is this, exactly?

  • Measured dataRecorded directly by an instrument at a real place and time. Nothing on this page is measured data.
  • Reanalysis dataGridded estimates built from models blended with real observations, used for climate analysis at scale.
  • Modelled dataOutput of a physical or statistical model, validated against real-world measurements.
  • Synthetic demonstrationPlausible, hand-built values used to illustrate a relationship. The dataset and sandbox above are this.
  • Scenario assumptionA stated simplification — like the offset values behind the sandbox's ground and shade options.
This sandbox is an educational model showing how environmental conditions can influence thermal stress. It is not a site-specific engineering or health assessment. Air temperature, surface temperature, Mean Radiant Temperature and UTCI are four distinct quantities — none of them substitutes for another.
